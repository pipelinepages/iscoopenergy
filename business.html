What the assessment considers

The load assessment sizes the system around how your business uses energy.

The 3 key objectives of a load assessment

01Inverter size (kW)

Set from the peak load expected to run at the same time.

02Battery size (kWh)

Set from the energy required during nighttime or other periods without solar generation.

03Solar panel size (kWp)

Set from total daily energy use and the site’s solar resource.

General data collected

A Load inventory

List every appliance and piece of equipment intended to run on the system. Record its rated power in watts (W) from the nameplate where available.

B Operational time

Estimate how many hours per day each load operates. This converts power to energy: Energy (Wh) = Power (W) × Time (h).

C Load classification

Resistive: heating elements and incandescent lamps. Record electronic loads such as TVs from their nameplates; they do not have the motor/compressor starting surge. Motors and compressors: air conditioners, refrigerators and pumps may draw inrush current around 3–7× rated power for a few seconds, depending on the equipment. Separate critical and non-critical loads for prioritisation.

D Demand analysis

Connected load: sum of rated loads. Peak demand: maximum load expected to operate simultaneously and a key input to inverter sizing. Apply a diversity factor based on realistic operating patterns, since not all loads run at once.

E Time and seasonal patterns

Separate daytime and nighttime energy use: daytime demand can be met by solar generation, while nighttime demand needs stored energy or another source. Account for seasonal changes in usage and generation.

F Losses and design margins

Include suitable allowance for future expansion and system losses. The supplied planning guidance uses a 20–25% buffer; final margins depend on the design. Account for inverter efficiency (commonly around 90–95%, model-dependent) and usable battery capacity based on the selected battery’s depth-of-discharge limits.
